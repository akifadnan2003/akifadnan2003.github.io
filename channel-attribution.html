Sales by Channel

For every product: which share of its sales came from ads, search, direct visits, email, referrals and AI assistants

Google Analytics 4 BigQuery SQL Next.js TypeScript Cloud Run
Back to Projects

Overview

Marketing reports often list dozens of traffic sources that nobody has grouped, and they answer nothing. The question leadership actually asked was simple: for each product, how much of its sales comes from ads, and how much comes on its own?

This app answers that. Every order is matched to the visit that led to it, the visit is put into one clear channel, and every product gets a single bar that shows its channel mix.

Channels

ChannelWhat counts
AdsPaid search, shopping and display campaigns
OrganicUnpaid search results and social posts
DirectTyped-in visits and searches for the brand name
EmailNewsletters and order or shipping emails
ReferralLinks from other sites, payment and review platforms
AI assistantsVisits from ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot and similar tools
UnknownKept as its own grey segment, never folded into another channel

What it shows

Products ranked by sales

Each product with a 100% bar across the channels, searchable, with a drill-down into its monthly trend and the raw source, medium and campaign behind it.

Signals

Flags such as ads-dependent, organic star or channel mismatch, so outliers stand out. An ads-only best seller may need content and SEO; a product that sells without ads raises the question of whether its ads pay off.

Ad spend against sales

Shopping campaign cost per product next to the sales those campaigns brought, with a plain label from losing money to strong.

Business customers

Each trade customer marked as new, growing, stable, declining, paused or lost, using the same fixed rules whatever the date filter.

Stores and countries

The same view by the store a customer bought from or the country the order was delivered to.

Order list and export

Every order with its channel and campaign, searchable and exportable to CSV.

How it works

Google Analytics 4 export (purchase events, last-click source) + order lines and customer data └── One reporting table in BigQuery, rebuilt daily after the sales data lands ├── Channel rules in a single SQL CASE, easy to change └── Check query: totals must match the sales records Web app (Next.js, TypeScript, Cloud Run, company single sign-on)

What I learnt

Attribution fields that look trustworthy often contradict each other. Going back to the raw analytics events, and keeping the raw values on every row, made it possible to explain every bar on the screen.

Unknown should stay visible. Hiding it inside Direct makes the chart prettier and the decisions worse.
